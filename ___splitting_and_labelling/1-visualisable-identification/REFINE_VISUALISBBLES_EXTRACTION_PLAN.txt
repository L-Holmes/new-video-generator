--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RULE THAT GOVERNS THE WHOLE PLAN: NO WORD IS EVER WRITTEN DOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not one of the fixes below may name a word. No "contested", no "worth", no
"about", no "one". A rule that names a word fixes one script and no other.

WHAT MAY BE WRITTEN DOWN INSTEAD -- types, never members:

    WordNet SUPERSENSES        noun.artifact, noun.attribute, verb.change,
    (lexnames)                 verb.communication ... 26 noun types and 15
                               verb types, the whole vocabulary sorted for us
    WordNet TAXONOMY NODES     property.n.02, quality.n.01, attribute.n.02 --
                               naming a branch, not its leaves
    WordNet ADJECTIVE CLASSES  adj.pert (relational: "volcanic"),
                               adj.ppl (participial: "contested")
    spaCy TAGS AND DEPENDENCIES  VBN, JJR, neg, mark, acomp, nummod
    BRYSBAERT THRESHOLDS       a number on a 1..5 scale, applied to every
                               word alike

    All of these are decided ONCE and then work on every word in English,
    including the ones no one has thought of.

THE ONE EXCEPTION, AND ITS LIMIT
    shared_text_logic.py already holds word lists -- WEAK_VERB_LEMMAS,
    WEAK_ADJ_LEMMAS, GENERIC_TOPIC_NOUNS, APPROXIMATOR_WORDS. They stay,
    because the sentence splitter and the tagger share them and deleting them
    would change those files' output.

    But NO NEW WORD MAY BE ADDED TO ANY OF THEM BY THIS WORK. Where an
    earlier draft of this plan said "add worth/worthy/due/aware to
    WEAK_ADJ_LEMMAS", that was wrong and has been replaced by a computed
    test (STEP 5b), which catches "worth" and everything shaped like it.

    Two of the existing lists are CLOSED GRAMMATICAL CLASSES -- determiners
    and APPROXIMATOR_WORDS (about / roughly / nearly / over). English has
    about a dozen of each and has not gained one in a century, so using them
    is closer to reading a tag than to writing a lexicon. Everything else --
    every noun, verb and adjective decision -- is computed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE IDEA BEHIND ALL SEVEN COMPLAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A SLOT IS A THING TO FILM. Everything else the sentence says about that thing
-- what it is doing, what it looks like, how many there are, whose it is --
is an ATTRIBUTE OF THE SLOT, and the Visualisable already has the fields:

        visualisable   the thing            "archipelago"
        action         what it does         "grew"          <- complaints 1, 5
        variant        what it looks like   "tiny, remote"  <- complaint 6
        amount         how many             1               <- complaint 5 (NEW)
        owner          whose it is          "the viewer"    <- complaint 2

    complaint                       mechanism                               step
    ------------------------------  --------------------------------------  ----
    1  "open"                       a verb goes in `action`, never a slot   1
    5a "grew"                       a verb goes in `action`, never a slot   1
    5b "one"                        a bare count goes in `amount`           4
    3  "about two dollars"          trim the edges of a search term         2
    6a "the Banda Islands"          trim the edges of a search term         2
    6b "tiny, incredibly remote"    a describing adjective goes in          3
                                    `variant`; only a KIND-forming one
                                    stays in the name
    4  "contested" / "worth"        is this word a picture at all           5
    4  "nutmeg's weight"            is this word a picture at all           5
    2  "the viewer's cupboard"      an owner you cannot film                6
    -  the next seven               negation, similes, comparisons ...      7

So the work is NOT "write better filters". It is: stop putting descriptions in
the slot list, put them in the fields that already exist, and answer two
genuinely new questions with WordNet -- "is this word a picture at all" (5)
and "does this adjective change the KIND of thing or only its LOOK" (3).

STEP 1 -- A VERB IS NEVER A SLOT. IT IS THE THING'S `action`.
         complaints 1 ("open") and 5a ("grew")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES: _visualisables_extractor.py, HOW_TO_USE_visualisables.py

WHAT
  KIND_ACTION and KIND_QUALITY stop being emitted as slots. They keep being
  HARVESTED -- the harvesters are what define a strong verb / strong
  adjective -- but they are dropped before the template is punched, with
  dropped_by="not-a-slot:action" / "not-a-slot:quality", so the working shows.

  Add next to CONCRETE_KINDS:

      # The kinds that earn a SLOT. Everything else is an attribute OF a
      # slot: a verb is the thing's `action`, an adjective its `variant`.
      SLOT_KINDS = CONCRETE_KINDS | {KIND_SOUND, KIND_REFERENCE,
                                     KIND_DEICTIC, KIND_FALLBACK}

  and a filter run AFTER resolve_overlaps(), so an adjective inside a noun
  phrase is still absorbed the way it is today, and only the ones that
  survived on their own are cut.

WHERE THE VERB GOES -- the whole point of the step
      "If you open your kitchen cupboard"
          [1] the viewer            deictic   action = "open"
          [2] kitchen cupboard      thing     action = "open"
      "Nutmeg only grew in one place"
          [1] Nutmeg                name      action = "grew"
  Both already happen today (measured). Step 1 only removes the duplicate.

THE ONE REAL CONSEQUENCE -- SEGMENTS THAT GO EMPTY
  "swerved after having" and "lifted out" have nothing BUT a verb. Today they
  get an action slot; afterwards nothing, and the mercy rule will not save
  them (LENGTH_FALLBACK_MIN_TOKENS is 4 real words).
  Do NOT lower that dial. In apply_length_fallback(), emit the KIND_FALLBACK
  when the segment's only survivors were actions/qualities, whatever its
  length: a segment that says only what is happening, with nothing to film,
  is exactly the "hold the picture you already have" case the fallback kind
  was invented for.

OPEN QUESTION FOR YOU -- `action` or `actions`?
  You wrote "the actions attribute". The field today is `action`, ONE string,
  and _verb_phrase() returns one verb plus its particle ("flew away").
  A thing can do two things in a segment: "It swished and swerved".
  Options: (a) keep one string and let it hold the coordination ("swished and
  swerved"); (b) make it a list. (a) is one line in _verb_phrase() and breaks
  no reader; (b) is truer but changes the output shape for everything
  downstream. RECOMMEND (a) now, (b) only if the renderer asks for it.

RISK
  HOW_TO_USE_visualisables.py's copied-in output changes and must be re-run
  and re-pasted -- that file's whole claim is that its output is real:
      "there's a [valley] [filled] with"  ->  "there's a [valley] filled with"
      "[valley] was once [covered]"       ->  "[valley] was once covered"

DONE WHEN
  * spices segment 1 reads "If [the viewer] open" with ONE slot, action=open.
  * spices segment 11 reads "[Nutmeg] only grew", action=grew.
  * no KIND_ACTION or KIND_QUALITY row appears in any slot table.
  * SUMMARY still says 0 segments with nothing to put on screen.

STEP 3 -- KIND vs LOOK: which adjective is part of the thing's NAME
         complaint 6b ("tiny, incredibly remote volcanic archipelago")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES: _visualisables_extractor.py (trim_to_searchable_core, Candidate,
       _candidate_to_visualisable, fill_variants)

THE JUDGEMENT, IN YOUR WORDS
      a VOLCANIC archipelago is a different THING from an archipelago
      a REMOTE archipelago is the same thing, differently

  So an adjective belongs in the search term only when it forms a KIND. When
  it only says how this one looks, it belongs in `variant`:

      "A tiny, incredibly remote volcanic archipelago"
          visualisable  "volcanic archipelago"
          variant       "tiny, incredibly remote"

WHAT DECIDES IT -- one WordNet class, no word list
  A RELATIONAL (pertainymic) adjective is one WordNet files as adj.pert: it
  means "of or pertaining to <noun>", it is derived from that noun, and it
  therefore names a SUBTYPE. Measured:

      adj.pert      volcanic, royal, solar, medieval    -> forms a kind
      adj.all       tiny, remote, old, huge, ancient, loud, black, yellow,
                    wooden, rusted, wrinkled, broken     -> describes a look

  That is the whole rule for adjectives:

      an adjective stays in the name IFF WordNet files any of its senses as
      adj.pert. Everything else moves to `variant`.

  COMPOUND NOUNS ARE NOT ADJECTIVES and always stay -- dep_ == "compound",
  "kitchen cupboard", "whale skulls", "spice trade". Two words, one thing.

AND THE FALLBACK YOU ASKED FOR, AS A DIAL
      KEEP_KIND_FORMING_ADJECTIVES = True
  Set it False and NO adjective ever stays in the name -- every one of them
  becomes variant. That is your instruction ("if we can't accurately identify
  that, just put all adjectives in the variant") made switchable rather than
  argued about. Measure the adj.pert rule on the five scripts; if it is
  wrong even once in a way that matters, flip the dial and delete the branch.

NOTHING IS LOST BY MOVING AN ADJECTIVE
  Say this in the docstring, because it is the reason the split is safe: the
  search layer can always put the two back together -- "yellow" + "paint" is
  still "yellow paint" when it wants a picture of one. What the split adds is
  the knowledge of WHICH half is the thing, so the shot can keep the thing
  and change the look when a later segment changes it.

CARRYING THE WORDS ACROSS
  trim_to_searchable_core() currently throws away what it cuts. It must keep
  it: Candidate.trimmed_description: list[str], copied onto the Visualisable
  and used by fill_variants() as a FOURTH source alongside the three in
  _variant_descriptions(). Left-to-right order, deduped by the existing
  per-identity accumulation.

  GATE IT THROUGH STEP 5, or the trimmings re-introduce exactly the words
  step 5 exists to keep out: step 5 refuses "contested" a slot, and this
  step would hand it straight back as a variant.

  Adverbs ride with their adjective ("incredibly remote", already what
  _adjective_phrase() does), so the variant reads like the script.

DONE WHEN
  * spices segment 14: "volcanic archipelago", variant "tiny, incredibly
    remote".
  * spices segment 6: "seed", variant "little, wrinkled".
  * tractor segment 8: slot "paint", variant "yellow" -- AND the tractor's
    own variant still accumulates "yellow paint" from the caused-motion rule,
    which reads the parse and is untouched. If that second half breaks, the
    step is wrong.
  * no visualisable in any slot table has more than one adjective in it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4 -- AMOUNT: a count is not a picture
         complaint 5b ("one")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES: _visualisables_extractor.py (Visualisable, as_dict, a new
       fill_amounts() in the document pass), HOW_TO_USE_visualisables.py,
       TEST_MANUAL_INTERPRETATION.py

THE FIELD
      amount: int = 1     # how many of `visualisable` are on screen
  Default 1 ALWAYS -- "a tractor" and "the tractor" are both one tractor. It
  joins as_dict() next to `owner`, and the test's slot table gets an AMOUNT
  column.

WHERE IT COMES FROM -- all off the parse, no word list
  a) A NUMERAL MODIFYING THE THING.  dep_ == "nummod"
         "three ships"  -> visualisable "ships", amount 3
         "900 ships"    -> visualisable "ships", amount 900
  b) A PARTITIVE.  NUM -> "of" -> the thing
         "One of them"  -> the slot is "them" (-> bees), amount 1
  c) NOTHING SAID -> 1.

WHAT STOPS BEING A SLOT
  A bare cardinal with nothing to count. Today "one" survives because it
  arrives as a CARDINAL entity (detector number:ent, measured) and the
  generic-noun rule that kills "one place" never sees it. The rule:

      a NUM candidate not glued to a MEASURE_NOUN or a currency is not a
      picture. Move it onto the thing it counts; if there is no such thing,
      drop it (dropped_by="count-without-thing").

  MONEY AND MEASURES KEEP THEIR SLOTS -- "two dollars", "15 metres",
  "$2 million", "30 percent" -- because there the number IS the picture, a
  counter or a bar on screen, which is what KIND_NUMBER was for. Their
  `amount` stays 1: one picture of "two dollars". Say that in the field's
  docstring or someone will read amount as "the number in the text".

OPEN QUESTION FOR YOU (do not guess)
  A bare plural with no numeral -- "the bees", "whale skulls". amount 1 is
  literally wrong; amount "many" changes the field's type. RECOMMEND: leave
  it 1, and add `plural: bool` off spaCy's Number=Plur if the renderer ever
  needs it. Cheap either way; a wrong guess is not.

DONE WHEN
  * spices segment 12 has no "one" slot at all.
  * bees segment 9 "One of them landed" -> one slot, "bees", amount 1.
  * whales keeps its money slot; the AMOUNT column is 1 everywhere else.

STEP 5 -- IS THIS WORD A PICTURE AT ALL?
         complaint 4 ("contested", "worth", "nutmeg's weight")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE: _visualisables_extractor.py (drop_abstract, plus one shared gate)

The only step that needs a new judgement rather than a new home for an old
answer. Three word classes, three computed tests, ZERO words written down.

--- 5a  NOUNS: WordNet's supersense says what TYPE of thing a word is -------
  Every noun in WordNet carries a lexname -- one of 26 supersenses -- which
  is a ready-made answer to "what kind of thing is this". Measured on the
  scripts' own nouns:

      noun.artifact    cupboard 4.79  windscreen 4.59  tractor 5.00
      noun.animal      bee 4.88          noun.plant     seed 4.71
      noun.object      archipelago 3.96  valley 4.72  planet 4.58
      noun.location    site 4.56  place 3.48        noun.person  viewer 4.13
      noun.phenomenon  light 4.21  sandstorm 4.63  wind 3.93
      noun.substance   wax 4.97          noun.group     hive 4.83
                       ^ all filmable, all already kept today

      noun.attribute   weight 3.94  size 3.13  majority 2.54
      noun.state       thing 3.17  freedom 2.34  monopoly 2.69
      noun.cognition   idea 1.61        noun.relation   percentage 2.90
                       ^ NONE filmable -- and weight 3.94, size 3.13 and
                         thing 3.17 all pass MIN_CONCRETENESS today, which
                         is exactly the "nutmeg's weight" bug

  RULE: a KIND_THING whose head noun's first sense is in

      NEVER_FILMABLE_SUPERSENSES = {"noun.attribute", "noun.cognition",
                                    "noun.feeling", "noun.motive",
                                    "noun.relation", "noun.state"}

  is dropped WHATEVER Brysbaert says. Everything else keeps today's
  behaviour (Brysbaert >= 3.0, then the physical/abstract WordNet backstop,
  then keep).

  WHY ONLY SIX SUPERSENSES ARE DECISIVE -- the rest go both ways, measured:
      noun.possession   gold 4.81 (film it)   resource 2.55 (do not)
      noun.quantity     dollar 4.93 (film it) most others (do not)
      noun.act          battle 4.00, war 3.63 (film them)  journey 2.57 (no)
      noun.event        accident 3.26         revolution 2.87
      noun.communication  letter 4.70, postcard 4.93, note 4.61, sign 4.62
                          (all real objects)  song 4.46, story 3.30 (not)
      noun.time         spring 3.89 (a coil, when it is not the season)
                        moment 1.61, history 2.96
  Brysbaert is the right arbiter for those, and already is. Do NOT make them
  decisive -- noun.communication would delete the clockmaker's postcards and
  note, and noun.time would delete a coil spring. Written down here so the
  next person does not have to rediscover it.

  KNOWN RESIDUAL, stated rather than hacked: "whale song" (4.46) survives.
  Brysbaert rates it like the letter it is not. Live with it until it hurts.

--- 5b  ADJECTIVES: three tests, in order -----------------------------------
  Brysbaert MUST NOT be used here. Measured, it does not separate the classes
  at all -- single 3.27 and famous 2.07 are not pictures, ancient 2.04 and
  old 2.72 are. There is no threshold. (Put those numbers in the comment at
  CONCRETENESS_APPLIES_TO, which already says so without evidence.)

  i)  PARTICIPLES -- decided by the verb underneath.
      An ADJ tagged VBN is a verb wearing an adjective's clothes, so ask what
      the verb does. Measured:
          contest -> verb.communication          rust  -> verb.change
          say     -> verb.communication          break -> verb.change
                                                 wrinkle -> verb.contact
      KEEP only if ANY of the verb's first three senses is in
          PHYSICAL_VERB_SUPERSENSES = {"verb.change", "verb.contact",
                                       "verb.body", "verb.motion",
                                       "verb.creation", "verb.weather"}
      Three senses, not one: "bury" is verb.perception first and verb.contact
      third, and "the site was buried" is a picture. "contested" has no
      physical sense at any depth. THIS is what drops "contested", with no
      word anywhere in the code.

  ii) ATTRIBUTE POINTERS -- what property does this adjective name?
      A WordNet adjective often points at the attribute it measures, and the
      TAXONOMY splits the physical ones from the evaluative ones. Measured
      paths, all under attribute.n.02:
          little->size, narrow->width, old->age, loud->volume(sound_property),
          black->value.n.04 (colour), broken->integrity, dead->animation
              ... all reach property.n.02 or state.n.02       -> A LOOK
          important->importance, single->individuality, afraid->fear,
          modern->modernity
              ... reach quality.n.01, trait.n.01, feeling.n.01 or
                  temporal_property.n.01                      -> NOT A LOOK
      RULE: drop when the attribute's path hits
          NON_VISUAL_ATTRIBUTE_ROOTS = {"quality.n.01", "trait.n.01",
                                        "feeling.n.01",
                                        "temporal_property.n.01"}
      keep when it hits property.n.02 or state.n.02 without one of those.
      13 of 13 test cases correct. Node names, not words.

  iii) RELATIONAL ADJECTIVES -- the ones that need a complement.
      "It was worth more than its weight in gold." "worth" states a relation;
      it does not say how anything looks. It has no attribute pointer and is
      not a participle, so (i) and (ii) both pass it -- but the PARSE gives
      it away. Measured:
          worth   acomp, subtree contains a `prep` ("than ...")   -> drop
          famous  acomp, no children                              -> keep
          loud    acomp, only an advmod ("really")                -> keep
          ancient amod,  no children                              -> keep
      RULE: an ADJ whose SUBTREE contains a prep / pcomp / ccomp, and which
      has no physical attribute pointer from (ii), is relational -> drop.
      Subtree, not children: spaCy hangs the "than" phrase off "more", not
      off "worth".
      Measure this one on all five scripts before turning it on. If it costs
      a real adjective, ship (i) and (ii) alone and leave (iii) written down
      as the reason they were not enough. Do NOT replace it with a list.

--- 5c  VERBS: the same supersense test, as a backstop ----------------------

STEP 7 -- THE ONES THAT WILL TRIP US UP NEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You asked what other TYPES of word will catch us out. These are the ones the
current results file already gets wrong or is one script away from getting
wrong. Each is a computed test; none needs a lexicon. (a) and (b) are real
bugs visible in TEST_RESULTS today and should be done with steps 1-6; the
rest are ordered by how likely they are to bite.

  a) NEGATION -- the picture is the OPPOSITE of the word.
     REAL BUG, bees segment 12: "it was not afraid of us" gives the slot
     variant="afraid". The script says it was NOT.
     TEST: a `neg` child (STL.NEGATION_TOKENS) on the verb or adjective that
     a variant/action is being read off.
     DO: drop the description rather than invert it -- "not broken" is not a
     picture of anything, it is the absence of a change. Also covers "no
     ships" (det "no") and "without water" (ADP), which are the same fact
     wearing different grammar.

  b) COMPARISON FRAMES -- the thing named is not on screen.
     REAL BUG, spices segments 9-10: "It was worth more than its weight in
     gold" makes GOLD the sentence's setting, so nutmeg and its weight are
     both filmed "in gold". There is no gold in this video.
     TEST: the candidate sits inside a `prep` subtree headed by a
     STL.COMPARATIVE_MARKERS member ("than"), or under a comparative
     tag (JJR/RBR).
     DO: keep the slot if you like, but it may never be a SETTING, and it
     should not carry the sentence's action. Simplest correct thing:
     classify_settings() refuses any candidate inside a comparison frame.

  c) HYPOTHETICALS, CONDITIONALS AND SIMILES -- it did not happen.
     whales: "The ground looked as if an ocean had simply dried up around
     them." There was no ocean.
     spices: "If you open your kitchen cupboard" -- the cupboard is
     hypothetical too, though harmless because it is the viewer's own.
     TEST: the candidate's clause hangs off a `mark` ("if", "as if", "as
     though", "like"), or its verb carries an irrealis modal aux (would,
     could, might) or a `neg`.
     DO: not a drop -- these are usually the most visual sentences in a
     script. Flag them: a `hypothetical: bool` on the Visualisable, so the
     renderer can style them (dream/sketch) instead of filming them as fact.
     RECOMMEND flagging only; deciding what to do with it is the renderer's.

  d) PARTITIVES AND CONTAINER PHRASES -- the head noun is not the thing.
     "one of them", "a handful of spices", "a lot of ships", "a series of
     raids". The picture is the OBJECT of "of", not the head.
     TEST: head noun's supersense is noun.quantity / noun.group / noun.
     attribute AND it has an `of` prep child with a noun object.
     DO: the same move as step 4b -- promote the object, keep the count in
     `amount`. Step 4 does the numeral case; this is the same rule for the
     non-numeral ones, so write them together.

  e) EVENT AND ACT NOUNS -- a story, not a shot.
     "journey" 2.57, "revolution" 2.87, "trade" 3.08, "voyage" 3.43. They
     name spans of time, not framings. Brysbaert already drops the low ones;
     the risk is the high ones ("battle" 4.00) which really are filmable.
     TEST: supersense noun.act / noun.event, already measured as ambiguous.
     DO: nothing yet. Watch it on the whales and rome scripts, which are full
     of them, and only then decide. Noted so it is not rediscovered.

  f) ROLE AND RELATION NOUNS -- "the owner", "a member", "the rest".
     They point at somebody without saying what they look like.
     TEST: supersense noun.relation, plus noun.person whose head is modified
     by an `of` phrase ("the owner OF the tractor" -> film the tractor).
     DO: resolve to what they relate to, the same way the possessives
     resolve. This is the wishy-washy-terms BONUS in myownstuff.py wearing a
     different hat, and the coreference cluster already contains the answer.

  g) UNITS AND BARE MEASURES -- "metres", "percent", "degrees" alone.
     Already half-handled: find_number_candidates() glues the measure to its
     number precisely so the noun-phrase harvester cannot emit it bare.
     TEST: MEASURE_NOUNS membership with no numeral attached.
     DO: drop. Cheap, and it is the one place a closed class is the right
     tool -- units are a finite list by definition.

  h) TIME-OF-DAY AND ERA NOUNS AS THINGS -- "at night", "in the 1600s".
     Dates already have their own kind and should keep it. The risk is
     "night" and "morning" arriving as KIND_THING settings.
     TEST: supersense noun.time on a KIND_THING candidate.
     DO: not a drop -- a night scene is a real look. Route it to `variant`
     of the SETTING ("valley" + "at night") rather than to a slot. Half of
     this already exists in fill_locations(); the other half is a decision
     nobody has made yet.

WHAT NOT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * DO NOT HARDCODE A WORD. Not "contested", not "worth", not "about", not
    "one". If a change names a word, it is the wrong change -- go back and
    find the TYPE it belongs to (supersense, taxonomy node, adjective class,
    tag, dependency, concreteness band). No new word may be added to any
    shared_text_logic list by this work either.
  * Do not use Brysbaert concreteness on adjectives or verbs. Measured above;
    it is why CONCRETENESS_APPLIES_TO exists.
  * Do not make noun.communication, noun.time, noun.act or noun.event
    decisive supersenses. Measured above: they would delete postcards, a
    coil spring and a battle.
  * Do not delete find_action_candidates() / find_quality_candidates() in
    step 1. They are the definition of "strong verb" / "strong adjective"
    that fill_actions() and the variant path lean on, and they keep the
    dropped-trail honest.
  * Do not rewrite the template text. The words on screen stay the script's
    words; only the SPAN a slot covers may shrink. (Same rule as the
    abstract-terms map: what the viewer hears is what goes on screen, and
    swapping the words would move every offset in the template.)
  * Do not add a second spaCy model or a network call. Every test above is
    the shared sm parse, Brysbaert, or a WordNet lookup already imported.
  * Do not lower LENGTH_FALLBACK_MIN_TOKENS to paper over step 1's empty
    segments. Emit the fallback for the right reason instead.
