There is a phone box standing in the middle of a field in Japan. It is not connected to anything. No line, no dial tone, no bill. Locals call it the wind phone. Since 2011, more than 30,000 people have travelled there to speak to someone who died. They pick up the handset, they say the thing they never got to say, and they hang up. Somebody wrote in the notebook beside it: "I am still setting a place for you at dinner." The phone has never rung back.
